About

Engineering for surgeons who are far from the specialist they need

I'm a biomedical and electrical engineer from Zimbabwe, now a PhD candidate in Biomedical Engineering at the University of Cape Town.

My research is on surgical telementoring: letting an experienced surgeon supervise and guide laparoscopic procedures remotely, with video, audio and on-screen annotation that arrive fast enough to be useful in theatre. The work sits where real-time video engineering, embedded measurement hardware and computer vision meet, and it is aimed at the shortage of specialist surgeons in remote and under-served regions. That goal lines up with UN Sustainable Development Goal 3: good health and well-being for everyone, wherever they live.

Before the PhD I spent several years hands-on with medical equipment: planning, installing, calibrating and servicing X-ray machines, dialysis units, autoclaves and chemistry analysers in hospitals across Zimbabwe. I also designed and installed solar power systems, which taught me to build things that keep working far from the nearest workshop.

Research focus

Three questions I keep coming back to

How do you mentor a surgeon in real time?

A WebRTC platform that streams the laparoscope and an external theatre camera to a remote mentor, with telestration drawn straight onto the surgical view and two-way audio.

How much delay is really there?

A glass-to-glass latency rig built around an ATmega328 microcontroller and light sensors, so delay is measured from the endoscope's view to the mentor's screen instead of estimated from network statistics.

Can software see trouble coming?

Models that anticipate intra-operative bleeding seconds before it starts, trained on laparoscopic cholecystectomy video, so a mentor can warn a trainee in time.

PhD research software

Laparoscopic Telementoring Platform

Active

Real-time telementoring web app with dual-camera streaming (laparoscope plus external view), interactive telestration with pen, shapes, arrows and undo/redo, two-way audio and session monitoring. Being extended with an AI voice assistant and bleeding-anticipation computer vision.

  • Next.js
  • React
  • WebRTC
  • Socket.IO
  • Tailwind

EndoLatency

v0.1

Desktop tool plus microcontroller firmware that measures the glass-to-glass latency of an endoscope video chain and of a telementoring link. An LED in the endoscope's field of view is flashed and light sensors on each screen time its arrival, so link delay needs no clock synchronisation between computers. Guides sensor placement, records trials, analyses them and exports a report.

  • ATmega328
  • TEMT6000
  • Python
  • PySide6

Bleeding Anticipation for Laparoscopic Surgery

Predicts that bleeding is about to start several seconds before it does, from laparoscopic cholecystectomy video. Compares a video-frame pipeline (ResNet18 + GRU) with a symbolic event-log pipeline adapted from a published Transformer architecture, under one strict definition of anticipation.

  • PyTorch
  • CNN + GRU
  • CholecTrack20
  • CholecT50

Telementoring Desktop (mentee side)

Operating-room-friendly desktop app that captures the laparoscope and an external camera, blurs faces in real time for patient and staff privacy, and serves as the base for WebRTC streaming and telestration overlays.

  • PySide6
  • OpenCV
  • MediaPipe

Theatre Recorder

In progress

Windows application that records surgical video from a USB capture device with an optional webcam picture-in-picture, through a single hardware-accelerated FFmpeg pipeline that writes crash-safe fragmented MP4.

  • FFmpeg
  • Intel Quick Sync
  • PySide6
